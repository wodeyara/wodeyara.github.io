I have been building this for a minute (with Claude's considerable help), and it is finally in a state worth sharing: <a href="https://www.anirudhwodeyar.com/fertilityAcrossWorld/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">the unexplained fertility explorer</a>.

It is a side project that lets anyone poke at a simple question: how well is fertility across the world actually explained by the predictors we usually reach for? You pick the factors — GDP per capita, female schooling, labour-force participation, contraceptive prevalence, child mortality, urbanisation, mobile subscriptions, social cohesion — and the app fits an ordinary least-squares model of log total fertility rate on whatever you selected, then maps the residual for every country. Red is more children than the model predicts, blue is fewer. The residual is the interesting part: it is the fertility your chosen story does *not* account for.

I also invented a new predictor, which I am calling the **Possibility Index**: a rough measure of the opportunity available to people in a country. It is a composite of the density of social and leisure amenities (from OpenStreetMap), internet and mobile penetration, population density, and net migration, z-scored and averaged. It is easily the most experimental thing in there, and the amenity coverage is patchy for the largest countries, whose OpenStreetMap queries time out and fall back to the other components.

The reason I wanted such a measure at all is the claim that keeps surfacing — that smartphone use has driven fertility down (see <a href="https://archive.is/3NHdU" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">this piece</a>). I read that less as phones-as-such and more as *imagined* opportunity: a phone is a window onto every life you could be living instead. Time substitution is surely part of it too. Either way, the connectivity factors and the Possibility Index are my attempt to get a handle on it — with the caveat, noted in the app, that mobile subscriptions and the internet component of the Possibility Index are collinear, so selecting both makes the individual coefficients hard to read.

A few things I tried to be careful about. Predictors get a data-chosen transform — a log where it straightens the relationship, a quadratic term where the shape is genuinely bent — rather than assuming everything is linear. Missing values are shown as "insufficient data" and never imputed. And pronatalist policy is an overlay, not a covariate: policy is usually a *reaction* to already-low fertility, so putting it in the model would buy you a nice piece of reverse causality. There is also a sub-national view for US states, fit as its own separate model with its own factors, because the covariates are not comparable across scales.

So: why is Kazakhstan so badly explained by every obvious index we have? I genuinely do not know. If you do, please tell me.

I should say plainly that I know very little about this area. Nearly everything I have picked up comes from podcast episodes on the topic. But fertility rate keeps coming up in the zeitgeist, and it does seem to be falling more or less everywhere, and I wanted a way to play with the data and ask my own questions of it rather than take someone else's summary on faith. Residuals describe association, not causation — that holds for everything in the app.

I enjoyed making it, and I hope someone else enjoys playing with it. If something does not make sense, or is simply broken, let me know.
